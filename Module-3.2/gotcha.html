What just happened

That email promised a free ticket. The form asked you to type in some details. This page was the "prize." None of it was real — it was a class exercise to see how a phishing link actually feels to click.

If this had been a real attacker, the details you typed into that form could already be in someone else's hands. That's the whole point of the drill.

The red flags you swam past 🐠

  • 1 Too good to be true. A free prize out of nowhere is one of the oldest bait tactics in the book.
  • 2 Urgency / excitement. "Claim it now" short-circuits the part of your brain that would normally ask questions.
  • 3 Asked for details before anything was proven. Legit prizes don't need your info before you've even seen what you won.
  • 4 A link led to another link led to another page. Real phishing chains hop across multiple sites to dodge filters — this one did too.
  • 5 No way to verify the sender. Did you check who actually sent the original email, or just trust the subject line?

Next time, before you click

Hover over links to see where they actually go. Check the sender's real address, not just the display name. Ask: would this organization really contact me this way, about this, right now? And if a message makes you feel a rush of excitement or panic — that feeling is the trap working, not a reason to hurry.
